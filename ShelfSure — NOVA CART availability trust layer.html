ShelfSure: one honest answer to “is it on the shelf?”

Three surfaces share one brain. Everything below is live. Tap in any screen and the others react. Press play for the guided story, or run it yourself: order the butter, then answer as the store.

Input

Store shelf-check taps, sales velocity from the order database, time since the last confirmation, each item’s past failure rate.

Processing

P(in stock) = (1 − e−stock ÷ (expected sales since check + 0.5)) × (1 − past failure rate, faded right after a check)

Scores decay as time passes and recover the moment a store taps.

Action

High confidence: confirm instantly. Middle: ask the store one question, in parallel. Low: hide it, offer a swap, and log the demand.

What it’s worth, and how we’ll prove it

Every baseline is from the brief. Every assumption is a slider rather than a hidden constant. Start with the expected case, then try to break it. The pilot at the end decides whether the money is released.

Assumptions

Cumulative value against the ₹25L budget

Method: revenue per order = ₹26.1L ÷ 38,500 = ₹67.8. Stock-driven cancellations = product unavailable (35% of cancellations) plus half of store rejections (18%, the rest assumed to be busy-hour capacity). Retention effect = a share of the 14-point repeat-rate drop recovered, applied to 46,000 active users. Support savings count missing-item, wrong-item and half of refund-status tickets. Partner value = stores at risk (18% of 620) retained, with half their volume assumed to shift elsewhere anyway. Effects are treated as additive and ramp over six months. The pilot exists to replace these assumptions with measurements.

Where the ₹25L goes

No warehouses, no hiring wave. The build reuses the partner dashboard, order database, coupon system and delivery tracking that NOVA CART already runs.

Rollout in four phases

1

Pilot, weeks 1–6

MVP engine, shelf check and customer badges on the existing apps. 60 stores with the highest stock-driven cancellations, plus 60 matched control stores.

2

Gate and tune, weeks 7–12

Read out the pilot. Tune thresholds and swap rules. If the gate is cleared, extend to about 250 stores and switch on store incentives.

3

Scale, months 4–5

All 620 stores. Reliability tiers feed search ranking. Unmet-demand tips go to stores.

4

Compound, month 6

Use the trusted catalogue: a multi-store “order three” journey, and promo budget moved from first-order discounts to it.

The pilot design

  • 60 treated stores against 60 matched controls, six weeks
  • Primary metric: stock-driven cancellations per 1,000 orders
  • Also tracked: second order within 30 days, tickets per 1,000 orders, store satisfaction

The go/no-go gate

  • Scale if stock-driven cancellations fall by 40% or more
  • Guardrails: delivery time no worse than +1 min, median store response under 45 s, checkout conversion not down more than 2%
  • About ₹9L is committed before the gate. The other ₹16L is released only if it clears.

What could go wrong

Stores won’t do itThe ask is two minutes a day, one tap per item, sorted by money at risk. Reliable stores earn search ranking and a lower commission tier from the ₹2.5L incentive pool.
Store checks slow down checkoutChecks run in parallel with the order, only for items below the threshold, with a 60-second timeout. Unconfirmed items are never charged.
Cold startDay-one scores use sales velocity and past failures from the order database, so the engine is useful before a single store taps.
GamingScores are weighted by outcomes. A store that says “in stock” and then rejects the order drops a reliability tier.

The prompt journey

Seven prompts shaped the diagnosis and the product. Each one changed a decision. Open a row to see what was asked, what it surfaced, and what it changed.

Is NOVA CART growing or buying revenue?Reconcile
“Reconcile the six-month table. Compute net revenue after promotion, the revenue earned per extra rupee of promo, and the acquisition cost per customer who reaches a second order. State every assumption.”
What it surfaced

Net revenue after promotion fell from ₹12.3L to ₹9.1L. Extra promo earned ₹0.57 per ₹1. A second-order customer costs about ₹502, roughly seven orders of platform revenue to earn back.

What it changed

“More acquisition” stopped being a candidate for the lead strategy. The finance head’s framing became the lens.

How can customers churn without rating us badly?Hypotheses
“61% of churned customers rated us 4★ or higher. List the failure modes that cause churn without a bad rating, and the data that would falsify each.”
What it surfaced

Failures that happen before or instead of a delivered order (cancellation, silent substitution, a refund that drags) never reach a rating prompt. The survey ranks availability, delay and price as the main complaints.

What it changed

We stopped looking at delivered-order experience and started looking at pre-delivery failure.

Which single upstream cause explains the most symptoms?Causal map
“Map every operational number in the brief to a root cause. Which one upstream cause explains the most downstream symptoms? Separate what is reported from what is inferred.”
What it surfaced

Stale inventory touches unavailable-product cancellations, store rejections, substitutions, 57% of tickets, partner effort and exit risk, and the 19% who search for items nearby stores lack.

What it changed

The problem became availability truth, not delivery speed, not promotions, not discovery.

What does each executive get right and wrong?Steelman
“Steelman each of the six leaders. For each: the strongest evidence for, what would disprove it, and whether it describes a symptom, an amplifier or a root cause.”
What it surfaced

Marketing is disproved at the margin. Operations is real but mostly downstream. The partner manager is nearest to the cause. The product head’s strategy depends on the same fix.

What it changed

It gave us the “who’s right” table, and a way to answer the CEO’s question with one product.

Which product should be built under ₹25L?Options
“Generate eight candidate products. Score each on root-cause depth, number of leadership claims resolved, fit with ₹25L and no new infrastructure, time to first impact, and store effort. Kill what fails.”
What it surfaced

Promo optimisers, ETA tools, support consoles and discovery engines all treat symptoms. The availability layer scored highest while staying inside the constraints.

What it changed

We committed to ShelfSure and wrote down what we were deliberately not building.

How do we keep store effort under two minutes a day?Design
“Design the confidence model so stores spend at most two minutes a day and customers are never charged for an item that isn’t there. What is the simplest formula, and how does it fail?”
What it surfaced

Expected sales since the last check plus last known stock gives a defensible probability. Ask stores only where uncertainty × sales × price is highest. Fail safe: if a store doesn’t answer, the item is not promised.

What it changed

It defined the engine, the three action bands, the 60-second timeout and the store’s ranked shelf-check list.

Which assumptions break the business case?Stress test
“Stress-test the ROI. Which assumptions matter most, and how do we turn them into a pilot with a go/no-go gate?”
What it surfaced

Repeat-rate recovery and store adoption dominate the result. Both are measurable within six weeks on a matched sample.

What it changed

Assumptions became sliders, the pilot gets a hard 40% threshold, and only about ₹9L is committed before it.
